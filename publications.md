
# Publications

![Publications Banner Image](./Img_D/publications_banner.jpg)

*11 peer-reviewed papers and book chapters, plus a 2026 deep-learning preprint. 380+ citations and an h-index of 8. Full, up-to-date list on [Google Scholar](https://shorturl.at/hYrV9).*

## AI & deep learning
1.  Srivastava N, Faber J, Srivastava DA (2026). An interpretable physics-informed multi-stream deep learning architecture for the discrimination between earthquake, quarry blast and noise. arXiv preprint. [arXiv:2602.15993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15993)
    *   A deep-learning model that tells earthquakes apart from quarry blasts and background noise using a single seismic station, with three parallel branches (waveform, spectrogram and physics features); 97.6% accuracy on the Pacific Northwest benchmark.

## Plant–microbe symbiosis & nutrition
2.  Das D, Tripathi S, Mutyala P, Srivastava DA, Bücking H (2023). Development and resource exchange processes in root symbioses of legumes. IntechOpen (book chapter). [doi:10.5772/intechopen.111540](https://doi.org/10.5772/intechopen.111540)
    *   How legumes host nitrogen-fixing rhizobia and arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi at once, and how the three partners trade nutrients for plant carbon.

## Plant pathogens & biocontrol
3.  Arya GC, Srivastava DA, et al. (2022). BcHnm1, a predicted choline transporter, modulates conidial germination and virulence in *Botrytis cinerea*. Fungal Genetics and Biology. [doi:10.1016/j.fgb.2021.103653](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fgb.2021.103653)
    *   Mutants lacking BcHnm1 take up less choline, germinate less and cause smaller lesions on tomato and bean leaves.
4.  Srivastava DA, Harris R, Breuer G, Levy M (2021). Secretion-based modes of action of biocontrol agents with a focus on *Pseudozyma aphidis*. Plants. [doi:10.3390/plants10020210](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants10020210)
    *   Review of how biocontrol agents protect crops through what they secrete: antimicrobials, enzymes, reactive oxygen and effectors that trigger plant resistance.
5.  Srivastava DA, Arya GC, et al. (2020). Transcriptome profiling data of *Botrytis cinerea* infection on whole plant *Solanum lycopersicum*. Molecular Plant-Microbe Interactions. [doi:10.1094/MPMI-05-20-0109-A](https://doi.org/10.1094/MPMI-05-20-0109-A)
    *   Public RNA-seq dataset of tomato and *B. cinerea* across five infection stages on intact plants.
6.  Arya GC, Srivastava DA, et al. (2020). A non-GPCR membrane-bound CFEM protein regulates plant pathogenicity and germination in *Botrytis cinerea*. Microorganisms. [doi:10.3390/microorganisms8071043](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms8071043)
    *   Deleting the CFEM protein cut spore germination by about 20–30% and slowed lesion spread on tomato leaves.
7.  Vela-Corcía D, Srivastava DA, et al. (2019). MFS transporter from *Botrytis cinerea* provides tolerance to glucosinolate-breakdown products and is required for pathogenicity. **Nature Communications**. [doi:10.1038/s41467-019-10860-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-10860-3)
    *   The transporter mfsG pumps toxic isothiocyanates out of the fungus, letting it infect glucosinolate-producing plants. 140 citations.
8.  Srivastava DA, Yakubov M, et al. (2018). Multiparametric analysis of diversity in *Botrytis cinerea* isolates from Israel. Phytoparasitica. [doi:10.1007/s12600-018-0684-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12600-018-0684-4)
    *   Isolates from different crops compared by DNA markers, growth, virulence and oxidative-stress tests reveal two distinct ways of causing disease.
9.  Maayan Y, Pandaranayaka EPJ, Srivastava DA, et al. (2018). Using genomic analysis to identify tomato Tm-2 resistance-breaking mutations and their underlying evolutionary path in a new and emerging tobamovirus. Archives of Virology. [doi:10.1007/s00705-018-3819-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00705-018-3819-5)
    *   Traces the origin of tomato brown rugose fruit virus, which broke Tm-2 resistance that tomato growers had relied on for 55 years.

## Crop genetics & genomics
10. Srivastava DA, Ben-Israel I, Zhu C, Nida H, Yu J, Fridman E (2019). Heterosis association mapping for grain quality and yield-related traits in a *Sorghum bicolor* diallel implicates prevalence of dominance complementation. In *Sorghum: Properties, Synthesis and Applications* (book chapter).
11. Nida H, Blum S, Zielinski D, Srivastava DA, et al. (2016). Highly efficient de novo mutant identification in a *Sorghum bicolor* TILLING population using the ComSeq approach. The Plant Journal. [doi:10.1111/tpj.13161](https://doi.org/10.1111/tpj.13161)
    *   Compressed sensing plus sequencing finds rare mutations in 1,024 sorghum lines using only 48 pooled samples.
12. Tamuli R, Kumar R, Srivastava DA, Deka R (2013). Calcium signaling. In *Neurospora: Genomics and Molecular Biology* (book chapter).
